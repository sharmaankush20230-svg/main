📸 How to take a good photo?

A good photo means a good check. Keep these 4 things in mind:

1. One leaf, close up

Do not take the whole plant. Keep one diseased leaf in the centre of the frame, so spots are clearly visible.

2. Take it in good light

Take it in daylight. Spots hide in dark or shadowy photos.

3. Straight, without shaking

Keep the phone steady. A blurry photo can give a wrong result.

4. Check both sides

If there are spots on the underside too, check that side in a separate photo.

🌿 Common Problems — General Info

This is only general information, not a diagnosis of any photo. For doses, ask a local agriculture officer.

Black/brown spots on leaf (Leaf Spot)

Round or irregular spots appear on the leaf and slowly grow. Remove the diseased leaf separately and keep the area around the plant clean.

White powder-like coating (Powdery Mildew)

A white powder collects on the leaf. Improve air flow and do not crowd plants too close together.

Tiny insects stuck on leaf (Aphids)

Tiny green/black insects stick to soft new leaves. A gentle stream of water can wash them off; if there are many, ask an expert.

Leaf turning yellow (Yellowing)

Too much or too little water, lack of sunlight, or lack of nutrition can also turn leaves yellow — it is not always a disease.

Plant wilting (Wilting)

If the soil is dry, water it. If it still wilts when the soil is wet, there may be a root problem — show it to an expert.

❓ Frequently Asked Questions

How long does a result take after taking a photo?

Usually a few seconds, depending on internet speed. If the key is wrong or there is no net, you will see an error — no fake result is made.

Is my photo or key uploaded anywhere?

The key stays only in your browser. The photo is sent only to the AI service for checking, and history is saved only on your device.

What if the result looks wrong?

Take another photo in good light, close up. Before deciding for a large crop, always ask a local agriculture expert.

Why is no chemical dose written?

Dose depends on crop, age and area. A wrong dose can cause harm, so no dose guess is given here — follow the packet instructions or an expert's advice.

🌿 More Common Problems

Roots rotting from too much water

If the soil stays wet for days and the plant wilts, the pot may not drain well. Let the soil dry a little between waterings and check that water can drain out.

Scorched patches from strong sun

Pale, dry patches on leaves that face harsh afternoon sun can be sunburn, not infection. Move a potted plant to lighter shade for a few days and watch it.

Mottled, crinkled leaves

A mosaic-like pattern with crinkled growth can spread through tools and hands. Do not take cuttings from such a plant, clean tools, and ask an expert before replanting nearby.
